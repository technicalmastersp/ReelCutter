Best clip length for Reels, TikTok & Shorts

Platforms keep raising their technical maximums, but the length that actually gets watched hasn't moved nearly as much. Here's what to aim for when you set up a split.

Every major short-form platform now technically allows much longer uploads than when Reels, TikTok, and Shorts launched. That doesn't mean you should use the full ceiling — discovery algorithms still lean hard toward shorter, tightly-paced clips. Here's the practical picture as of 2026, and how to translate it into settings for the split tool.

Instagram Reels

Instagram now allows Reels up to around 20 minutes when uploading a pre-recorded file, up from a 90-second cap just a couple of years ago. In practice, though, Instagram's discovery algorithm favors much shorter runtimes for reach to non-followers — commonly cited sweet spots are roughly 7–30 seconds for broad discovery and up to about 60–90 seconds for tutorial-style content. Runtimes beyond a few minutes tend to reach mostly your existing followers rather than new viewers.

TikTok

TikTok supports much longer uploads than it started with — up to several minutes when recording in-app, and considerably longer for uploaded files. Even so, TikTok's own historical engagement data has repeatedly pointed to short clips (well under a minute) performing best for completion rate and replays, which are strong ranking signals on the platform.

YouTube Shorts

YouTube Shorts allows videos up to about 3 minutes, a significant jump from its original 60-second limit. Average watched Shorts still tend to run closer to 20–40 seconds, and anything under roughly 5 seconds is unlikely to gain traction.

A practical rule of thumb

Goal Suggested length
Maximize reach / new viewers7–30 seconds
Tutorials / how-to steps30–90 seconds
Deep dives for existing followers90 seconds–3 minutes
Cross-posting the same cut everywhere15–60 seconds (safest across all three)

Note that platform limits and algorithm preferences change fairly often — treat the numbers above as a snapshot rather than gospel, and check each platform's current help pages before planning a long-running content strategy around them.

Setting this up in Reel Cutter

If you're chopping one long recording into a batch of clips rather than editing bespoke short-form content, pick a min/max range that matches your slowest platform's discovery sweet spot — for cross-posting, something like 60–90 seconds per part is a reasonable middle ground. For a tutorial-style series meant to be watched in order by existing followers, the default 150–160 seconds works well.
